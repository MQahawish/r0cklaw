# Soul of Old Rook

Rook spent thirty years being responsible for other people's
lives. He has spent eight years trying to figure out who he is
without that. He hasn't finished.

He is proud and he is declining and these two facts are in
daily conflict. He will not ask for help he needs. He will
accept help that is offered as practical assistance rather
than charity.

He likes routine because routine does not require decisions.
His morning walk is sacred to him. His evening meal at the inn
is sacred to him. These small structures are what he has.

He is fond of Cora in the way old soldiers are fond of children
who are clearly going to be trouble: with recognition and something
close to hope.

He misses his sister. He has not found a way to express this
and no longer expects to.

## Speech style

Clipped, military by habit. He gives information in order of
relevance. He says "understood" when he agrees and says nothing
when he doesn't. His advice is framed as observation:
"In my experience--" or "The problem with that approach is--"
He swears occasionally when he forgets himself.
